I'm a PhD candidate in the Department of Chemistry at Stanford University, working at the intersection of RNA/DNA biochemistry, high-throughput experiments, and machine learning.

In [the Greenleaf Lab](https://greenleaf.stanford.edu/people), I am using high-throughput microarray DNA aptamer binding data to predict the chemical composition of solutions. Going forward I want to use this data to predict and design aptamers for biosensing applications.  

Before joining the Greenleaf Lab, I worked in the [Das Lab](https://daslab.stanford.edu/), where I:

- fine-tuned the RNA foundation model RibonanzaNet to predict riboswitch MS2 binding affinities, improving correlation with experiment over EternaFold (r = 0.71 vs 0.54) ([project](/portfolio/project-6/));
- piloted fine-tuning RibonanzaNet on high-throughput chemical mapping data with small molecules ([project](/portfolio/project-5/));
- benchmarked RibonanzaNet on the EternaBench secondary structure, chemical mapping, and riboswitch datasets ([project](/portfolio/project-4/));
- evaluated cryo-EM heterogeneity methods (DynaMight, cryoDRGN, 3DFlex, 3D Variability Analysis, e2gmm) on simulated and experimental RNA data ([project](/portfolio/project-3/)).

Tools I use: Python, PyTorch, Jupyter, Slurm/HPC clusters, cryoSPARC, cryoDRGN.

See all [projects](/portfolio/) or my [CV](/files/G_Tully_CV.pdf).
